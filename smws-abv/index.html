How strong is an SMWS bottle?

1,709 UK Scotch single-cask releases, 2021 to October 2026 · by Blair Milligan · figures last changed 2026-10-22

Scotch Malt Whisky Society releases are bottled at cask strength, straight from a single cask. We looked at the stated strength of 1,709 UK Scotch single-cask releases since January 2021. A reader asked whether SMWS bottles run unusually strong; here is what the data says.

The short answer: the median bottle is 58.5% ABV. Half of all releases sit between 56.6% and 60.6%. About a third (32%) are 60% or stronger, but only 5% reach 63.5%, a common strength for filling casks. So most bottles come out below a typical filling strength, as you would expect after years in the cask.

Each extra few years in the cask costs a point or so of strength, as alcohol evaporates through the wood.

Stronger bottles don’t cost more

Within the same age, median release price of the weakest, middle and strongest third of bottles by strength:

AgeWeakest thirdMiddle thirdStrongest third
10 to 12 years£65£65£64
13 to 15 years£80£80£78

Price follows age, not strength. If you like your whisky strong, the extra alcohol is free.

Sherry casks run very slightly lower

At the same age, sherry-cask bottlings are about half a point lower than bourbon-cask ones: 58.8% against 59.4% at 10 to 12 years, 57.5% against 58.0% at 13 to 15 years.

No change over time

The median was 58.6% in 2021 and 58.4% in 2026.

How this was worked out

UK releases only, 70cl, single casks of Scotch whisky (malt and grain); bundles, gift sets, and the Society’s rum, gin and non-Scotch releases excluded. Medians throughout. ABV is the strength stated by the Society. 63.5% is a widely used cask-filling strength in the industry, not a figure the Society publishes for its own casks. Every bottle has its own page here, with its strength compared against other casks of the same age, reached from its distillery code.
